What We’ve Heard From Neighbors

People are asking hard questions about water, bills, jobs, and quality of life. These are fair questions. We’ve collected some of the most common concerns below so everyone can see them and compare them with the facts.

“My bills are already drowning me.”

We get it. The price of power and water keeps climbing. The data center does not pull from your neighborhood’s electric or water service. Rates are set by utilities and reviewed by state regulators, not by one project.

Learn more about utility rates

“They’ll drain our water.”

Older data centers used huge amounts of evaporated water. Ours uses a closed-loop system that circulates the same water again and again. Yearly use is closer to a small office than a factory.

See how cooling works

“Only ten people will work there.”

Construction will employ hundreds of skilled workers. Long-term operations create fewer positions but at higher wages, with benefits and technical training. These are stable, career-path jobs—not temporary warehouse shifts.

Read about local hiring

Top Questions

These are the issues we hear most often. Click below for answers supported by studies, letters, and verified data.

Question

Will my power bill go up

Answer

AES Indiana confirmed that the project will not raise household rates. State regulators review major upgrades in open hearings. Sabey pays for its own dedicated infrastructure.

Read the AES Will Serve letter
Question

Will this use too much water

Answer

The cooling system is closed-loop, meaning the same water circulates continuously. Aside from restrooms and minor landscaping, annual use is similar to a small office building.

Cooling system reference Citizens Water letter
Question

Will noise or traffic affect us

Answer

Day-to-day operations are quiet with limited truck activity. The new plan cuts traffic by about eighty percent compared with the previously approved warehouse project. Backup generators are enclosed and tested only during the day.

View the traffic study

AES Indiana: No Negative Impact Expected To Existing Rates

Your power rates are set by the utility and reviewed by the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission in public. AES Indiana has said this project is not expected to raise existing customer rates.

AES

Public Statement

September 19, 2025

“We have a strategy that shows no negative impact to existing customer rates should AES Indiana serve data centers in the future. We can spread fixed costs over more electricity sold.”

Public Oversight

Major utility investments go through IURC review with public input and transparent filings.

Who Pays For What

Sabey pays for customer-specific and dedicated facilities plus required contributions. Households are not charged for those.

Fixed Cost Sharing

More power sold helps spread fixed costs across a larger base, which can help steady bills for everyone.

Design Choices That Reduce Impact

The design focuses on quiet operations, low visibility, and limited resource use. Every element is chosen to fit naturally into the surrounding area.

Closed-Loop Cooling

One-time initial fill, then office-like annual water use. No evaporation towers or high daily demand.

Shielded Lighting

Dark-sky fixtures prevent upward light and protect night skies. Security lighting uses motion sensors.

Landscape and Buffers

Native plantings, berms, and mature tree preservation soften views and protect residential sightlines.

Stormwater Management

Multiple engineered basins collect and filter runoff before it enters local waterways.

Why Decatur Township

The location has existing industrial zoning, nearby utilities, and access to skilled trades. It also allows space for wide landscape buffers and thoughtful design.

Skilled Local Workforce

Partnerships with local unions and schools create training pathways into high-wage technical careers.

Responsible Development

Data centers have low traffic and no smokestacks or process emissions, reducing neighborhood impact.

Tax Base Growth

New property and equipment taxes help fund schools, roads, and emergency services long-term.

Dedicated Utility Infrastructure

A separate AES substation and upgraded lines serve the project without pulling from local circuits.
