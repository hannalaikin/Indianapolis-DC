Common Questions & Answers

It's natural to have questions about a project of this scale. Here are the three questions we hear most often, answered directly with links to official documentation.

Question

"Will my power bill go up because of this?"

Answer

Existing customer rates are not expected to be negatively impacted per AES's plan and IURC-reviewed process. Sabey pays 100% of customer-specific and dedicated facilities and applicable riders. New load adds utility revenue that helps spread fixed costs across all customers, which can actually help stabilize rates.

Read the AES Will Serve Letter (PDF)
Question

"Will data centers use massive amounts of water from our community?"

Answer

No. Our closed-loop cooling system is designed for exceptional water efficiency. The system requires a one-time initial load of 1,000,000 gallons (500,000 per building, phased with Building A in 2028 and Building B in 2030). After that, annual water usage is comparable to a typical office building—approximately 200,000 to 300,000 gallons per year total for both buildings. This covers restrooms, kitchen facilities, landscaping, and humidity control. The previously approved Decatur Tech Park would have required significantly more water.

View Cooling Technology Details (PDF) Read Citizens Water Will Serve Letter (PDF)
Question

"Will noise and traffic disrupt our neighborhood?"

Answer

No. Operations are designed to be quiet and low-traffic. Independent analysis shows approximately 80% fewer peak-hour vehicle trips than a previously approved industrial plan. Generators run briefly for testing and during rare utility outages, typically about 20 hours per year total. Equipment is enclosed and acoustically treated; exterior lighting is shielded.

AES Indiana: “No Negative Impact Expected to Existing Rates”

We support public oversight. AES Indiana has stated their strategy anticipates no negative impact to existing customer rates, subject to IURC review. Here’s how that works.

AES

From the AES Indiana President

September 19, 2025

"We've developed a strategy that shows no negative impact to existing customer rates should AES Indiana power data centers in the future. Even though more investments are required to serve those customers, we will be able to spread our costs over a larger amount of electricity sold."

Data centers pay 100% of customer-specific infrastructure and applicable riders. Spreading fixed costs across more electricity sold can help stabilize rates for everyone, with IURC oversight.

Transparent Public Process

IURC (state regulators) review rate cases and major capital programs in public. This adds independent oversight to protect customers.

Data Centers Pay Their Share

Sabey funds customer-specific facilities and pays contributions for any upstream reinforcements attributable to our load.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my electric or water utility bills increase because of this project?+
No. Utility rates are determined by AES Indiana and the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission. Sabey pays for its own infrastructure and riders, and the additional power demand spreads fixed costs over a larger customer base, which helps stabilize rates rather than increase them.
How much water will the data center actually use?+
The system uses closed-loop cooling, which recycles water. After an initial fill of 1,000,000 gallons (phased across both buildings), annual use is comparable to an office building — roughly 200,000 to 300,000 gallons per year.
How many jobs will this project actually create for our community?+
Hundreds of union construction jobs during building, plus 50–75 high-skill permanent positions averaging $80K/year. Each role supports additional local jobs in trades, maintenance, food, and services — a 6–8× multiplier.
Will this increase traffic congestion or create noise?+
No. Peak-hour traffic is 80% lower than the previously approved warehouse plan, and acoustic enclosures limit sound during testing to meet local codes.
Is “AI” itself the risk here?+
Data centers don’t “run” AI robots — they store and process data for hospitals, schools, businesses, and first responders. The question isn’t whether data centers exist, it’s how responsibly they’re built. Ours is designed with strict environmental limits and transparency.
Why no vote? Why wasn’t this announced sooner?+
The site is already zoned industrial, allowing development by right. What’s different here is we’re opening the process early — hosting public meetings, publishing all documents, and committing to ongoing transparency.

Project Details at a Glance

Campus Layout

Building A: one story (~572,500 SF, ~30’ tall).
Building B: two stories (~490,000 SF, ~50’ tall).
Dedicated AES substation (~10 acres). Controlled access on SR-67; emergency/construction routes separated.

Neighborhood Buffers

200’ landscaped buffer at residential edges, preserved mature trees where possible, and visual screening berms with fencing to reduce sightlines and light spill.

Infrastructure Improvements

>$5M targeted road work and safety upgrades near the site; coordinated intersection improvements; sidewalk and trail links. These commitments are in writing and tracked publicly.

Timeline (subject to utility/IURC scheduling)

Permits & site prep begin 2026; phased build 2–3 years. AES anticipates initial upgrades by Q4 2027 with capacity ramping over ~5 years.

Design Choices to Minimize Impact

Closed-Loop Cooling

One-time initial fill, then office-like annual water use. No evaporative towers pulling millions of gallons.

Shielded Lighting

Dark-sky friendly fixtures, zero uplight, and cut-off shields to protect neighboring homes.

Landscape & Berms

Native plantings, tree preservation, and berms to soften views and provide seasonal color.

Engineered Stormwater

Multiple basins sized to modern standards — slowing, filtering, and protecting local waterways.
